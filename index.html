How Life works

The grid is a world of squares. Each square is either alive (glowing) or dead (dark). Every generation, all squares look at their 8 neighbors, the squares touching them on the sides and corners, and change at the same moment by three rules.

The three rules

SurvivalA live square with 2 or 3 live neighbors stays alive.

LonelinessWith 0 or 1 live neighbors, a live square dies.

CrowdingWith 4 or more live neighbors, a live square dies.

BirthA dead square with exactly 3 live neighbors comes to life.

The outlined square in each picture is the one being decided. The grid wraps around: a square on the right edge counts the left edge as its neighbor, so patterns that drift off one side come back on the other.

Controls

  • Play / Pause runs the world. Step moves forward one generation. The space bar also plays and pauses, and S or → steps.
  • Speed sets how many generations run each second, from 1 to 60.
  • Tap or drag on the grid to draw live squares. Start a drag on a live square to erase instead.
  • Add a pattern places a famous shape in the middle of the grid.
  • Random fills about a quarter of the squares. Clear empties the grid and pauses.
  • Grid − / + changes how many squares fit across (16 to 120). Your pattern stays centered.
  • Gen counts generations since the last Random or Clear. Alive counts live squares.
  • Color shows age: new squares glow mint green and turn blue the longer they survive.

The patterns

Glider
Five squares that walk diagonally across the grid forever.
Spaceship
A slightly bigger traveler that moves straight sideways.
Glider gun
A machine that fires a new glider every 30 generations.
Pulsar
A large symmetric shape that flashes through 3 forms and repeats.
Pentadecathlon
A bar that cycles through 15 shapes before repeating.
R-pentomino
Just 5 squares, but it churns for over a thousand generations before settling.
Acorn
7 squares that grow into a sprawling colony with several gliders.
Diehard
Busy for exactly 130 generations, then disappears completely (on a big enough grid).

Things to try

  • Draw a straight line of 3 squares: it becomes a blinker, flipping between flat and upright.
  • Draw a 2 × 2 square: a block never changes.
  • Fire the glider gun on a small grid and watch the gliders wrap around and crash into it.

Invented by mathematician John Conway in 1970.
